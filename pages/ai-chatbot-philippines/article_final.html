1. What does an AI chatbot do for a Philippine business?

An AI chatbot reads what the customer typed, finds the answer in the knowledge you gave it, and replies in your tone. Unlike a button-only bot, it copes with one-line questions such as “How much?” and “Still available?”, with mixed English and Filipino, and with questions asked in an order you did not plan for.

Most Philippine businesses sell through a Facebook Page and Messenger first, so that is usually where the chatbot lives. The same instruction and knowledge can then serve Instagram and a website chat. The work the chatbot takes on is narrow and repetitive on purpose: prices, availability, branches, booking steps, order status and first-level qualification. Refunds, complaints and negotiations stay with a person, with a clear handoff. I explain the handoff rules in When Should an AI Chatbot Hand Off to a Human?

I build on the platform that fits the process: Botcake with Pancake for Messenger-first teams, ManyChat for Instagram, GoHighLevel for CRM and booking, and PayMeGPT when the agent needs tools and a larger knowledge base. 3. How does the project run?

The project runs in six stages. The first two involve no software at all, and they decide most of the result.

  1. Diagnose. We agree on one business goal, write down the current process as it really is, and find where it breaks. The method is in Don't Automate Chaos.
  2. Design. I map the conversation, the handoff points and the data the chatbot must capture.
  3. Build. I write the instruction, prepare the knowledge base and connect your CRM, calendar or sheet.
  4. Test. I run the test set and fix root causes until it passes. The routine is in How to Test an AI Chatbot Before It Talks to Your Customers.
  5. Launch. Where the risk is high, the AI drafts and your staff send. Tasks graduate to full automation once they prove reliable.
  6. Review. Real conversations are read every week and the knowledge base is corrected. See the weekly review routine.
Dijae's rule

If you cannot test it, do not ship it. A chatbot that sounds good in a demo and a chatbot that is safe with your customers are different things, and only a written test tells them apart.

5. What must a chatbot pass before launch?

A chatbot is ready when it meets acceptance criteria you agreed on in advance. This is the checklist I use. Every line is pass or fail.

CheckPass means
AccuracyEvery answer in the test set matches your knowledge base. No invented prices, dates or policies.
Unknown questionsWhen the answer is not in the knowledge base, it says so and offers a person.
HandoffComplaints, refunds and requests for a person reach staff, with the conversation attached.
Lead captureName, need and contact details land in the CRM or sheet for every qualified chat.
Tone and languageReplies are short, polite and match the language the customer used.
PrivacyIt asks only for the data it needs and tells customers how it is used.
GuardrailsIt refuses off-topic requests and attempts to change its rules.

Success metric: after launch, track first-response time, the share of chats resolved without staff, and qualified leads captured per week. If you collect customer data, read the Data Privacy Act guide for chatbots, and for the refusal rules see AI agent guardrails.

An honest limitation: an AI chatbot can still be wrong, and it cannot rescue an unclear offer or a slow sales team. It answers faster and more consistently than a busy inbox, but I do not promise sales, and I will tell you when your process is not ready to automate.

6. What should you ask any chatbot provider?

Whether you hire me, an agency or a freelancer, ask these questions before you sign anything.

  1. Who owns the accounts? The Page connection, the platform account and the knowledge base should be in your name.
  2. How is it tested? Ask to see the test set and the pass criteria, not a demo.
  3. What does the monthly fee cover? Platform, AI usage, maintenance hours and changes should be listed separately.
  4. When does a person take over? The handoff rules should be written down.
  5. What happens to customer data? Where it is stored, who can see it, and how long it is kept.
  6. Can your team update it? You should be able to change a price without a support ticket.

If you need one simple bot quickly, a freelancer may be enough. If you need a large team for a long enterprise project, an agency fits. Frequently asked questions

What is an AI chatbot?

An AI chatbot is software that reads a customer's message, finds the answer in the business knowledge it was given, and replies in natural language. It handles questions a button-only bot cannot, and hands off to a person when needed.

Can an AI chatbot understand Taglish?

Yes. Modern AI models handle Taglish and common Filipino shorthand well when they are given good examples and instructions. It should still be tested on real customer messages before launch.

How much does an AI chatbot cost in the Philippines?

It depends on five lines: setup, the platform plan, AI usage, maintenance and channel fees. A focused chatbot for one job costs far less than a full system with CRM and follow-up. I scope it on a free call.

Which channels can the chatbot work on?

Facebook Messenger, Instagram and website chat are the most common. The channel is chosen after the process is mapped, because it depends on where your customers already message you.
