1. Why do customers miss appointments?

Most no-shows are not customers who decided to waste your time. They forgot, their plans changed and cancelling felt like more effort than not showing up, or the details were unclear and they never checked again. The longer the gap between booking and the visit, the more room there is for all three.

That is why a reminder system has three jobs, not one:

  1. Remind: put the date, time and place in front of the customer again, close enough to the visit to matter.
  2. Confirm: ask for a reply, so you know who is coming and who is silent.
  3. Release: make rescheduling or cancelling a one-tap action, so a slot that will be empty comes back to you early enough to fill it.

A reminder that only does the first job helps. A system that does all three turns a surprise gap in your day into a slot you can offer to someone else.

2. What does the research say about reminders?

Reminders are one of the best-studied tools in scheduling, mostly from healthcare, where missed visits are tracked carefully. Three sources are worth knowing:

  • Cochrane review, 2013. Across 7 randomized trials with 5,841 participants, attendance was 78.6% with text message reminders against 67.8% with no reminder. Phone call reminders reached 80.3%, and texts and calls performed about the same. In two of the studies, the cost per attended appointment was 55% and 65% lower with texts than with calls.
  • BMJ Open meta-analysis, 2016. Pooling 21 studies, attendance was 67% with digital notifications against 54% without, and no-shows were 15% against 21%. Sending multiple notifications improved attendance more than sending a single one.
  • Randomized trial at Kaiser Permanente Colorado, 2018. 54,066 patients across 25 primary care clinics got one reminder 3 days before, one reminder 1 day before, or both. Missed visits were 5.8%, 5.3% and 4.4%. Two reminders beat one, and satisfaction did not drop.

These studies come from clinics, but the mechanism is general: people forget, and a timely nudge with an easy way to respond changes behavior. A small 2026 study at a single outpatient clinic in Texas points the same way: moving reminder calls from the day before to three or more days ahead, across 653 visits, lowered the no-show rate from 29% to 21%. Treat a single-clinic result as a signal, not a guarantee.

Dijae's rule

The first reminder is for remembering. The second is for deciding. Send both, and make "I can't come" as easy to say as "I'll be there."

3. When should reminders go out?

Start with a schedule that matches the evidence, then adjust it with your own data:

  1. At booking: confirm the date, time, place, what to bring and how to reschedule. Add a calendar link so the appointment lives in the customer's own calendar.
  2. About 3 days before: ask for a reply: confirm or reschedule. This is the message that frees slots early enough to refill.
  3. 1 day before: a short reminder with the time and address or video link. No new questions.
  4. No reply after the 3-day message: a person calls or messages directly. Silent customers are the ones most likely to miss the visit.
  5. Same-day appointments booked less than 3 days out: skip straight to a confirmation and one reminder a few hours before.

Keep it to these messages. More is not better once the customer has confirmed; every extra message is a chance for them to mute you.

5. What should a reminder say?

A reminder is read in two seconds on a lock screen. Put the time first, name the business, and give one clear action. Copy these and replace the brackets:

BOOKING CONFIRMATION
Hi [Name], you're booked with [Business] on [Day, Date] at [Time].
Where: [Address or video link]
Bring: [What to bring, if anything]
Need to change it? Reply RESCHEDULE or use [link].
Add to your calendar: [calendar link]

3 DAYS BEFORE
Hi [Name], a reminder from [Business]: [Day] at [Time].
Reply 1 to confirm or 2 to reschedule.

1 DAY BEFORE
See you tomorrow at [Time], [Name]. [Address or link]. Running late? Reply here.

NO REPLY (sent by a person)
Hi [Name], this is [Staff name] from [Business]. Just checking you can still make [Day] at [Time]. If not, no problem, I can move it for you.

Three details make the difference. The reply options are numbers, so they work on any phone. Rescheduling is offered in the same breath as confirming, so nobody has to feel awkward. And the no-reply message comes from a named person, because silence after an automated message usually means the customer needs a human.

Do not put sensitive details in a reminder. A clinic reminder should say the time and the place, not the treatment. Anyone can read a lock screen.

6. How do you handle cancellations and repeat no-shows?

A cancellation 3 days out is a win: it is a slot you can still sell. Build the system so every release goes somewhere useful:

  • Waitlist: when someone reschedules, offer the freed slot to the next person on a waitlist automatically, first to reply gets it.
  • Reschedule, don't just cancel: offer two or three new slots in the same message, so a "can't make it" becomes a moved booking instead of a lost customer.
  • Repeat no-shows: tag customers who miss without notice. A person reaches out before their next booking; your policy decides what happens on the second or third time.
  • Deposits and fees: some businesses take a deposit or charge a late-cancellation fee. That is a policy choice, not an automation. If you use one, state it plainly at booking and in the confirmation, never for the first time in a reminder.

If calls are a big part of your bookings, an AI voice receptionist can take reschedule requests by phone too. For a full clinic flow from inquiry to review, see AI automation for clinics and med spas.

7. How do you know the reminder system is working?

Measure your no-show rate for at least four weeks before you switch reminders on, so you have a baseline. Then track these every week:

MetricHow to calculateWhat it tells you
No-show rateMissed without notice ÷ booked appointmentsThe headline number. Compare it with your baseline.
Confirmation rateReplies of "confirm" ÷ 3-day reminders sentWhether customers read and act on the reminder.
Early release rateReschedules or cancels 24+ hours ahead ÷ bookingsWhether gaps reach you in time to refill.
Refill rateReleased slots rebooked ÷ released slotsWhether the waitlist is doing its job.
Delivery failuresFailed or bounced messages ÷ sentBad numbers, missing consent or channel problems.

Before launch, run this test with five real bookings on staff phones:

  • Each message arrives at the right time, in the customer's time zone.
  • Replying 1 marks the booking as confirmed in your calendar or CRM.
  • Replying 2 offers new slots and moves the booking without a person.
  • A booking with no reply creates a call task for a named staff member.
  • A cancelled booking stops all remaining reminders and alerts the waitlist.
  • No reminder shows treatment or other sensitive details.

Accept the system when all six pass. Review the numbers weekly for the first month, then monthly.

An honest limitation: reminders reduce no-shows, they do not remove them. Most of the research comes from healthcare, results vary by business and customer group, and some missed visits come from causes a message cannot fix, such as transport, cost or a booking made too far ahead. Use your own baseline, not someone else's numbers, to judge the result.

Frequently asked questions

How many appointment reminders should I send?

Two before the visit is a strong default: one about 3 days before that asks the customer to confirm or reschedule, and a short one the day before. A 2018 randomized trial found two reminders reduced missed visits more than either one alone. Add a booking confirmation and a call for anyone who does not reply.

Are text reminders as good as phone calls?

For most appointments, yes. A 2013 Cochrane review found text and phone reminders performed about the same for attendance, and texts cost less per attended appointment in the studies that measured cost. Keep calls for customers who do not reply.

Can I send appointment reminders through Facebook Messenger?

Only within limits. Meta retired the CONFIRMED_EVENT_UPDATE message tag and two others on April 27, 2026, so you cannot rely on Messenger for a reminder several days after the last message. Collect a phone number or email at booking and send reminders there.

What is a good no-show rate?

It depends on your industry, customers and how far ahead people book, so there is no universal target. Measure your own rate for four weeks before launch and judge the reminder system by how far it moves that number.
