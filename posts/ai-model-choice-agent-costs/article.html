The 60-second brief

  1. Within one provider, the top model can cost several times more per token than the small one. Most business chats do not need the top model.
  2. Route by job: small, fast models for routine replies; a larger model only for the hard cases.
  3. Caching repeated instructions and batching non-urgent work cut the bill further, without changing a word the customer sees.

Decision: default to the smallest model that passes your test set, and pay for more intelligence only where the answer needs it.

Move 01 · Diagnose

Sort the jobs before you pick a model

What

List what the agent actually does: answering FAQs, qualifying leads, summarizing chats, drafting replies, reasoning over several tools.

Why it matters

One model for everything means paying top-tier prices for “what are your opening hours?”

Do this

Tag each job by difficulty and by volume. The model choice follows from those two tags.

In most business chatbots, the bulk of messages are routine: prices, hours, availability, booking, order status. A smaller share needs real reasoning, such as comparing options, handling a complaint or planning several tool steps. The goal is to price each group separately.

Move 02 · Match

Match each job to a model tier

What

Providers sell model families in tiers: a small, fast model, a mid-size model and a top reasoning model.

Why it matters

Price rises steeply between tiers, especially for output tokens.

Do this

Start every job on the smallest tier and move up only when the test set says so.

Two details matter for budgeting. First, output usually costs much more than input, so long answers are expensive answers. Second, models and prices move fast. Gemini 4 Argon, the newest model on the list, is priced at $2.00 input and $10.00 output per million tokens through November 30, 2026, doubling to $4.00 and $20.00 from December 1, 2026. Gemini 3.8 Flash doubles from January 1, 2027. OpenAI follows the same pattern: its API has moved on to a GPT-6 family with a top model (GPT-6 Astra), a mid model (GPT-6.1 Sol) and a small model (GPT-6 Luna). For reference, check the pricing page here. OpenAI and Anthropic publish the same kind of tiered price lists; check their current rates here and here.

Move 03 · Route

Route to the small model first, then escalate

What

A routing rule sends each message to the cheapest model that can handle it.

Why it matters

High-volume, simple messages make up most of the bill, so they set the cost.

Do this

Escalate to a larger model only for named cases: complaints, multi-step tool work, low-confidence answers.

Simple escalation rules work well: hand the message to the larger model when the small one says it is unsure, when the customer is upset, or when the job needs more than one tool. Everything else stays on the small model. The same triggers often decide when a person should step in, as covered in the handoff playbook.

Move 04 · Optimize

Cut the bill without cutting quality

What

Four levers: caching, batching, shorter prompts and capped answers.

Why it matters

None of them change what the customer reads.

Do this

Turn on caching for your fixed instructions and knowledge, and batch anything that can wait.

Move 05 · Prove

Prove the choice with numbers

What

Run the same test set on each candidate model before you switch.

Why it matters

A cheaper model that answers wrongly costs more in lost sales and corrections.

Do this

Compare pass rate and cost per resolved conversation, not price per token.

Success metric: cost per resolved conversation, meaning total model cost divided by conversations that ended with an answer or a booked next step, with the pass rate on your test set held at or above today's level. The test method is in how to test an AI chatbot, and the weekly review that catches drift after a switch is in AI agent monitoring after launch.

Decision checklist before you switch models
Jobs are tagged by difficulty and volume
The small model passes the test set for routine jobs
Escalation rules are written and tested
Caching is on for fixed instructions and knowledge
Non-urgent jobs run as batch
Cost per resolved conversation is tracked weekly

An honest limitation: model names, prices and quality change every few months, and a public benchmark says little about your customers' questions. Treat any model choice as a decision you re-test each quarter, and re-check the price list before you budget.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best AI model for a business chatbot?

For most routine customer questions, a small, fast model from a major provider is enough. Use a larger reasoning model only for complex cases, and choose based on your own test set rather than public rankings.

How can I reduce AI agent costs?

Route routine messages to a small model, cache the instructions and knowledge sent with every message, batch non-urgent jobs, shorten prompts and cap answer length. Then track cost per resolved conversation.

Why do output tokens cost more than input tokens?

Providers price generated text higher than text you send. On the Gemini API price list, output tokens cost several times more than input tokens on every tier, so long answers raise the bill quickly.

How often should I review my AI model choice?

Review it at least every quarter and whenever a provider changes prices or releases a new model. Re-run your test set before switching.
