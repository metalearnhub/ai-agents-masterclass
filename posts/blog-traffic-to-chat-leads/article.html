1. Why blog traffic leaks

Search traffic is the most qualified traffic most small businesses will ever get. The reader typed their own problem into a search box. Nobody interrupted them with an ad. When your post answers that problem well, you have their attention at the exact moment they care.

Then most posts waste it in three ways:

  • No next step near the answer. The call to action sits only at the very bottom, after the reader has already found what they needed and left.
  • A next step that is too big. "Book a call" is the right ask for someone ready to buy. For someone still learning, it is a wall.
  • A chat that starts cold. If the reader does click to chat, the bot greets them with a generic menu, as if they had never read anything. They have to explain themselves again.

So why does chat fix this better than a contact form? Because a chat can ask one easy question, answer back immediately, and move at the reader's pace. A form asks for everything up front and then goes silent.

2. The path from search to booked call

Think of each post as the first message of a sales conversation, not as a brochure. The path has six steps, and every step has one job.

1Search. The reader types a problem. Your post title and description match the words they use, not your internal jargon.

2Blog post. It answers that exact problem, fully. Holding back the answer to force a chat backfires: people leave and do not come back.

3Chat button. A clear button near the answer, with an m.me link that carries a ref value for this post.

4First message. The chat opens with a message about the post they just read, not a generic menu.

5Two questions. Fit and timing, nothing more. You can learn the rest later. See how to qualify leads in Messenger automatically for the full method.

6Next step. Ready buyers get a booking link or a person. Everyone else gets something useful and a follow-up.

Dijae's ruleMatch the next step to the reader's intent, not to your sales target. A reader who searched "what is" wants to learn, so offer a checklist or a short answer in chat. A reader who searched "price", "cost" or "near me" is comparing options, so offer a quote or a call. One post, one intent, one next step.

3. Where the chat buttons go

First, one fact that changes older advice: Meta retired the Facebook Messenger chat plugin for websites, the small chat bubble many sites used to embed, on May 9, 2024. Older guides that tell you to install it are out of date. The reliable way to start a Messenger conversation from your website today is an m.me link on a button or text link.

I recommend three placements per post, each with its own ref value so you can see which one works:

PlacementReader's stateButton text that fits
Right after the key answerJust got value, wants to apply it"Get the checklist in Messenger"
Next to the price or setup sectionComparing options, has a specific question"Ask about your setup"
End-of-post boxRead everything, deciding"Talk to us on Messenger"

On mobile, a button the reader can tap with one thumb matters more than anything clever. Keep the button text about the reader's benefit, not about your channel: "Get the checklist" beats "Message us".

Watch outDo not put a chat button above the answer. Asking for a conversation before you have helped feels like a pop-up, and people scroll past it or leave.

4. Build m.me links with a ref

An m.me link opens a conversation with your Facebook Page. Add a ref value and the conversation carries context about where it started, such as a link on your website versus a link in a store. The format is simple:

https://m.me/YOUR-PAGE-NAME?ref=blog-cod-orders-mid
https://m.me/YOUR-PAGE-NAME?ref=blog-cod-orders-end

Two behaviors to know. When the person has never messaged your Page, Messenger shows that they came through a link and your Get Started step passes the ref along. When they already have a conversation with you, the link opens that same thread, still passes the ref, and resets the 24-hour standard messaging window so you can reply. My naming convention for ref values, so the reports stay readable a year from now:

blog-[post-slug]-[placement]
placement = mid | price | end

Examples:
blog-ai-chatbot-cost-price
blog-stop-bogus-cod-orders-mid
blog-ai-chatbot-human-handoff-end

Use only lowercase letters, numbers and hyphens, and keep each value short. Then add normal UTM parameters to the links that bring people to the post itself (from your newsletter or social posts), so your site analytics can tell you which channel sent the reader. 5. The first message

The first message decides whether the reader stays. It should prove you know why they came, offer what you promised on the button, and ask one easy question. Here is a copy-ready script for a post about stopping bogus cash-on-delivery orders:

Message 1 (sent when ref = blog-stop-bogus-cod-orders-mid):
Hi {first_name}! You were reading our guide on stopping bogus COD orders.
Here is the order-confirmation checklist from the post: [link]

Message 2 (one question, buttons):
Quick question so I can point you to the right setup:
How many COD orders do you ship per week?
[Under 50]  [50 to 300]  [Over 300]

Message 3 (based on the answer):
Under 50   -> send the free setup guide, tag "blog-lead-small"
50 to 300  -> ask: "Do you already use Pancake?" then offer a call
Over 300   -> tag "blog-lead-priority", assign to a person, offer a call today

Keep the AI agent on standby for questions in between. If someone types a question instead of tapping a button, let Botcake AI answer from your knowledge base and then bring them back to the next step. If the question needs a person, hand off cleanly; the human handoff playbook covers the triggers and setup.

Then follow up. A reader who started a chat but did not book is a warm lead, and a short, useful follow-up inside Meta's messaging rules often matters more than the first message. See how to automate lead follow-up with AI.

6. Measure it and test it

You need five numbers, and they live in different places. That is the part most teams get wrong: they look at blog views in one tool and chats in another and never connect them.

MetricFormulaWhat it tells you
Chat click rateChat-button clicks / post viewsWhether the button matches the reader's intent
Chat start rateChats started / chat-button clicksWhether the link and first message work on all devices
Qualification rateQualified leads / chats startedWhether the post attracts the right readers
Booking rateBooked calls / qualified leadsWhether the offer and follow-up are strong enough
Leads per postQualified leads per post per monthWhich posts deserve updates and more promotion

Do not compare your rates with numbers from other companies. Compare each post with itself month over month, and compare placements on the same post with each other. The ref values make that possible.

Acceptance test before a post goes live with chat buttons:

  • Every button opens Messenger on desktop, on iPhone and on Android, from a logged-in and a logged-out browser.
  • Each ref starts the right flow. Test from a Facebook account that has never messaged the Page and from one that has.
  • The first message names the post the reader came from.
  • Each answer to the qualifying question lands on the right tag and the right next step.
  • A typed question gets a correct AI answer, and "talk to a person" reaches a person.
  • The click shows up in your site analytics and the chat shows up in Botcake with its ref.

An honest limitation: m.me links do not pass the ref in every case. Meta states that refs might not work for some Messenger for Android users or for Pages with geographic restrictions. The link still opens the conversation, so build the flow to ask one quick "What brought you here today?" question whenever the ref is missing, and expect a small share of your chats to arrive without one.

Frequently asked questions

Can I still add the Facebook Messenger chat bubble to my website?

No. Meta retired the Facebook Messenger chat plugin for websites on May 9, 2024. Use m.me links on buttons instead, or a separate website chat tool if you need chat on the page itself.

What is a ref in an m.me link?

A ref is a short value added to an m.me link, such as ?ref=blog-pricing-end. It tells your Messenger bot where the conversation started, so Botcake can start a different flow for each post or placement.

How many chat buttons should a blog post have?

Usually three: one right after the key answer, one next to the price or setup section, and one in the end-of-post box. Give each one its own ref so you can see which placement works.

How do I know which blog posts bring leads?

Track chat-button clicks in your site analytics and chats per ref in Botcake, then tag qualified leads. Count qualified leads per post per month and update the posts that bring the most.
