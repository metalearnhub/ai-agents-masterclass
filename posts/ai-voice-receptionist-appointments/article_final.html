1. What an AI voice receptionist does (and does not do)

An AI voice receptionist is an AI agent on a phone line. It listens, understands what the caller wants, answers from your business knowledge and takes action in your tools: it checks the calendar, books or moves an appointment, sends a confirmation and transfers the call when a person is needed.

Good jobs for itKeep with a person
Booking, rescheduling and cancelling appointmentsMedical, legal or financial advice
Hours, location, parking, prices from your price listComplaints and refunds
After-hours and lunch-time callsAnything that needs judgment or an exception
Taking a clear message with a callback timeCallers who ask for a person

The receptionist should be honest about what it is. Callers accept an AI that books quickly and transfers smoothly. They do not accept one that pretends to be human and then gets stuck.

2. How a call flows

Every voice receptionist, whatever the platform, is built from the same five parts: a phone number, speech-to-text that turns the caller's voice into text, an AI agent that decides what to do, tools it can use (calendar, CRM, SMS) and a voice that speaks the reply. The quality of the call depends on the weakest of the five.

Two parts decide most of the caller's experience. The first is speed: a long pause after the caller stops talking feels broken on a phone, even when the answer is right. The second is the transfer: when the agent hands the call to a person, the caller should hear what is happening, and the person picking up should get a short summary so the caller does not repeat everything. Voice platforms support this. ElevenLabs agents, for example, can transfer a call to a phone number with one message read to the caller and a separate briefing for the person who answers. Dijae's rule

A voice agent should do one of three things on every call: book, transfer or take a clear message with a callback time. A call that ends with none of the three is a lost customer.

4. What to check before launch

Most voice agent failures are not about the AI. They come from settings nobody tested: a time zone, a buffer, a transfer number that rings a phone nobody carries.

Before you add outbound calls

Answering calls and making them are treated differently. In the United States, the FCC ruled in February 2024 that AI-generated voices count as “artificial” voices under the Telephone Consumer Protection Act, so outbound AI calls need the called person's prior express consent unless an exemption applies. For reference, check the summary here. In the Philippines, call recordings and caller details fall under the Data Privacy Act; the basics are in the Data Privacy Act guide for chatbots.

5. The 10-call test script

Run these ten calls yourself, from a phone that is not on your staff list, before the number goes live. Write down what happened on each one.

VOICE RECEPTIONIST TEST SCRIPT
1. Book the next available slot for your most common service.
2. Reschedule that booking to another day.
3. Cancel it.
4. Ask the price of a service that is on your price list.
5. Ask about something that is NOT in the knowledge.
6. Ask for a person during business hours.
7. Ask for a person after hours.
8. Play an upset caller with a complaint.
9. Call from a noisy place and speak fast.
10. Stay silent for 10 seconds, then hang up.

Pass when:
- Calls 1-3 match the calendar exactly (date, time, length, time zone).
- Call 4 gives the price on your list; call 5 says it will check, not a guess.
- Calls 6 and 8 reach a person, or end with a message and a callback time.
- Call 7 takes a message and states the callback time.
- Calls 9-10 end politely, with no booking made by mistake.

An honest limitation: voice is harder than chat. Heavy accents, background noise, poor signal and callers who talk over the agent still cause misunderstandings, and phone numbers and call costs differ by country. Start with booking and simple questions, keep a person reachable, and widen the agent's job only after the test calls and the weekly numbers say it is ready.

Frequently asked questions

What is an AI voice receptionist?

It is an AI agent that answers your business phone line. It understands what the caller needs, answers from your business information, books or changes appointments in your calendar and transfers the call to a person when needed.

Can an AI receptionist book appointments directly into my calendar?

Yes. Voice agents connected to a calendar, such as Google Calendar, can check availability and book, reschedule or cancel. Set the appointment lengths, hours, time zone, buffer and conflict calendars before launch, and test every change.

Should an AI receptionist tell callers it is an AI?

Yes. Say it in the first sentence. Callers accept an AI assistant that books quickly and transfers smoothly, and disclosure avoids the trust problem of a bot that pretends to be human.

Is it legal to make outbound calls with an AI voice?

In the United States, the FCC ruled in February 2024 that AI-generated voices are artificial voices under the TCPA, so outbound calls need prior express consent unless an exemption applies. Check the rules in each country you call, and get legal advice for outbound campaigns.
