1. The five cost lines

Every chatbot bill, on any platform, is made of the same five lines. Write them down before you compare vendors, because a cheap plan with expensive maintenance is not cheap.

Cost lineWhat it isHow it is billed
PlatformThe chatbot or CRM tool itself.Monthly or yearly plan, often tied to contacts, pages or seats.
AI usageThe language model that writes the replies.Per token, from a wallet or included in the plan.
SetupInstructions, knowledge files, flows, integrations and testing.One-time: your own hours or a done-for-you project.
MaintenanceReading conversations, updating prices and promos, retesting.Staff or freelancer hours every month.
Channel feesSMS, email or calls sent through the platform.Per message or per minute, where they apply.

For most Messenger and Instagram chatbots, the AI usage line is the smallest. Maintenance time is usually the largest recurring cost, and it is the one most quotes leave out.

2. What one AI reply actually costs

AI models are billed in tokens, small chunks of text. Every reply has two parts: the input (your instructions, the knowledge the AI pulls in and the conversation so far) and the output (the reply itself). Input is usually much larger, because your full instruction is sent with every single reply.

In a Botcake AI test on GPT-4.1 mini, one reply used 3,136 input tokens and 58 output tokens, and Botcake showed an estimated cost of $0.0013. You can check that against the model's list price: GPT-4.1 mini is priced at $0.40 per million input tokens and $1.60 per million output tokens.

Cost per reply = (input tokens  x input price  / 1,000,000)
               + (output tokens x output price / 1,000,000)

Example (GPT-4.1 mini):
  3,136 x $0.40 / 1,000,000 = $0.00125
     58 x $1.60 / 1,000,000 = $0.00009
  Cost per reply            = about $0.0013

Two things follow from this math. First, a shorter, cleaner instruction lowers the cost of every reply. Second, a larger model multiplies the whole bill, so move up a model only when testing shows the smaller one gives weak answers.

A chatbot that nobody reviews gets worse every time a price, promo or policy changes, and the cost of one wrong answer to a buyer is far higher than a month of AI usage. 5. Costs people forget

  • Long instructions. Every extra paragraph is sent with every reply. Keep long lists in knowledge files instead.
  • Longer conversations. The conversation so far is part of the input, so later replies in a long chat usually cost more than the first one.
  • Bigger models. Switching to a larger model raises the cost of every reply, not just the hard ones.
  • Contact overages. On contact-based plans, a successful ad campaign can push you into a higher tier.
  • Channel fees. SMS and calls are billed per segment or minute on platforms that send them.
  • An empty wallet. On pay-as-you-go AI, a balance that hits zero stops replies mid-conversation. Turn on auto top-up.

6. Cut cost without hurting answers

  1. Let keyword flows answer the simple questions first. Fixed replies for hours, location and payment methods cost nothing in AI usage.
  2. Start on a mini model and move up only when tests show weak answers.
  3. Trim the instruction. Follow a clear structure, like the one in How to Write AI Agent Instructions for Any Business, and remove repetition.
  4. Train the answers that matter so the AI gets them right the first time instead of across several back-and-forth replies.
  5. Cap automatic follow-ups at one or two. Each follow-up is another paid reply.

7. Cost check before launch

Treat cost like any other launch requirement. These are the acceptance criteria I recommend before a chatbot goes live:

CHATBOT COST CHECK
[ ] Average cost per reply measured on 20+ real test questions
[ ] Replies per conversation estimated from real chat history
[ ] Monthly AI usage = volume x replies x cost per reply
[ ] Total stays within budget at 2x expected volume
[ ] Platform tier checked against expected contacts or messages
[ ] Auto top-up on, with a balance that covers a busy weekend
[ ] Review owner named, review hours booked every month
[ ] Cost per conversation added to the monthly report

After launch, compare actual cost per conversation with your estimate every month. The 5 Chatbot ROI Metrics That Matter shows how to put that cost next to the value the chatbot creates.

An honest limitation: the $0.0013 figure is one measured reply on one instruction and one model. Your instruction length, model choice and conversation style will change it, and vendors change both token and plan prices. Measure your own cost per reply in testing and redo the math whenever a price changes.

Frequently asked questions

How much does an AI chatbot cost per month for a small business?

Add four lines: the platform plan, AI usage, maintenance time and any channel fees. At 3,000 conversations a month, AI usage on a mini model can be around $25, while staff review time and the plan price are usually larger.

How much does one AI chatbot reply cost?

It depends on the model and on how many tokens each reply uses. In a Botcake AI test on GPT-4.1 mini, one reply with 3,136 input tokens and 58 output tokens cost about $0.0013.

Is a free chatbot plan enough?

For many small pages, yes, as long as the free tier covers your volume. Botcake's Starter plan includes 500,000 automated messages a month. AI replies and staff time still cost money.

Why is maintenance part of the cost?

Prices, promos and policies change. Someone has to read conversations, update the instructions and knowledge files, and retest. Without that time, answers drift out of date and the chatbot starts losing sales.
