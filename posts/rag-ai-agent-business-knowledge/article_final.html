Ask a general AI model about your return policy and it will answer with confidence. The answer will sound right, read well, and have nothing to do with your actual policy. The model learned from the public internet. It has never seen your price list, your delivery areas or the exceptions your team makes every day. Retrieval-augmented generation, usually shortened to RAG, is how you make an AI agent answer from your business knowledge instead of from a good guess.

1. Why agents make things up

A language model is trained to produce the most likely next words. When it knows the answer, likely and correct are the same thing. When it does not, likely still sounds correct. That is why a model with no access to your business will invent a delivery fee, a store hour or a warranty term rather than say “I do not know”.

So why does this matter? Because in a customer conversation, a confident wrong answer is worse than no answer. The customer acts on it, your team has to correct it, and trust drops on both sides.

RAG fixes the cause instead of the symptom. The research paper that named it, by Patrick Lewis and colleagues in 2020, described models that combine what the model learned in training with a searchable store of documents it can look things up in. For a business, that store is your own knowledge.

2. How RAG works in plain English

Think of an open-book exam. Without RAG, the agent answers from memory. With RAG, it first looks up the right pages in your book, then answers using those pages.

Two things follow from this picture. First, the agent can only be as right as the passage it finds, so the search step matters as much as the model. Second, you do not need a bigger model to fix most wrong answers. You need better files and a better search.

Why not paste everything into the prompt? For a small business with one page of facts, that can work. As the knowledge grows, it stops working well. Research by Nelson Liu and colleagues in 2023 found that models use information at the start and end of a long input best, and perform noticeably worse when the relevant part sits in the middle. A focused search that hands the model the three right passages beats a wall of text.

3. Where each fact belongs

Most knowledge problems are not search problems. They are filing problems: the same price lives in the instructions, in two files and in an old website page, and they disagree. Decide where each fact lives by how often it changes.

WherePut hereDo not put here
InstructionsRole, tone, rules, what to never say, when to hand offPrices, stock, long policy text
Knowledge filesPolicies, FAQs, product and service details, price lists with a dateAnything that changes during the day
Live toolsStock, bookings, order status, customer account dataStatic policies that never change
Dijae's ruleOne fact, one home. Every price, policy and promise lives in exactly one place, and that place has an owner and a last-updated date. When the agent gives a wrong answer, you should be able to find the one line to fix in under a minute.

4. Prepare knowledge the agent can use

A search system does not read your files the way a person does. It splits them into small passages and finds the passages closest to the question. A passage that only makes sense next to the paragraph before it will often be found without its context, or not found at all.

Research on this exact problem backs the point. Anthropic reported in September 2024 that adding a short piece of context to each passage before indexing cut failed retrievals by about 35%, about 49% when combined with keyword search, and about 67% when the results were also reranked. You do not need to build that pipeline to use the lesson: write every section so it stands on its own.

  • One topic per file. “Delivery and shipping”, “Returns and refunds”, “Product: Model X”. Not “Everything 2026 final v3”.
  • Headings shaped like questions. Customers ask questions, so the search matches question-shaped headings better.
  • Repeat the subject. Write “Delivery to Cebu takes 3 to 5 days”, not “It takes 3 to 5 days”.
  • Date your numbers. Every price and fee gets “as of” and a date.
  • Say what you do not do. “We do not deliver outside the Philippines” prevents a whole class of invented answers.

A template you can copy for each knowledge file (every detail below is an illustrative example):

FILE: Delivery and shipping
OWNER: [Name]    LAST UPDATED: [Date]

## Do you deliver to [area]?
Yes. We deliver to [areas]. We do not deliver to [areas].

## How long does delivery take to [area]?
Delivery to Metro Manila takes [X] business days.
Delivery to provinces takes [X] to [Y] business days.

## How much is the delivery fee? (as of [Date])
Delivery to Metro Manila costs [amount].
Orders above [amount] get free delivery.

## What if my order is late?
If an order is more than [X] days late, the agent hands
the customer to a person on the team.

## What we do NOT promise
We do not promise same-day delivery.

6. Test the retrieval

If you cannot test it, do not ship it. Build a test set of 30 real questions, taken from your inbox, not invented by you: 20 your files answer, 5 your files do not answer, and 5 tricky ones (two products with similar names, a policy with an exception, a question in a different order of words).

Acceptance criteria before the agent goes live:

  • Grounded accuracy: at least 19 of the 20 answerable questions are correct and match the file they should come from.
  • Honest gaps: all 5 unanswerable questions get “I do not have that information” plus a handoff, with zero invented answers.
  • No contradictions: the tricky questions return the right product or exception, not a blend of two.
  • Freshness: change one price in a file, re-run the price questions, and confirm the agent uses the new price.

After launch, the metric to watch is the share of conversations where a person had to correct the agent, tracked weekly. If it rises, look at the files first, not the model. The full method is in how to test an AI chatbot before it talks to your customers.

An honest limitation: RAG reduces invented answers, but it does not remove them. The search can return the wrong passage, and the model can still misread the right one. That is why the test set includes questions your files do not answer, and why every customer-facing agent needs a clear handoff to a human.

7. Keep it current

Knowledge goes stale quietly. Nobody announces that a delivery fee changed; the agent just starts being wrong. A simple monthly routine prevents most of it:

  • Review every file with an owner. Update the date even when nothing changed, so you know it was checked.
  • Read the unanswered questions. Every “I do not have that information” is a candidate for a new entry.
  • Re-run the test set after any change to files, instructions or the model.
  • Remove before you add. Delete the old file when you upload a new version, so two versions never compete.

Frequently asked questions

What is RAG in simple terms?

RAG, or retrieval-augmented generation, means the AI first searches your own documents for the passages that answer a question, then writes its reply using those passages. It is like an open-book exam for the AI.

Do I need RAG if my business is small?

If all your facts fit on one or two pages, putting them directly in the instructions can be enough. Once you have several policies, products or price lists, knowledge files with retrieval give more accurate answers and are easier to keep up to date.

What file format works best for an AI knowledge base?

Plain, well-structured text works best: one topic per file, question-shaped headings and short self-contained answers. Botcake AI accepts Docx, PDF, TXT, XLSX and MD files up to 15 MB.

Is RAG the same as training or fine-tuning a model?

No. RAG does not change the model. It gives the model your documents to look up at the moment it answers, so you can update the knowledge by editing a file.
