What should a chatbot's first message say?

Short answer: who is talking, what it can do right now, and two to four taps for the most common reasons people message you.

“How can I help you?” feels polite, but it hands the hardest job to the customer: working out what to ask and how to phrase it. A good opener does that work for them. It names the assistant honestly, lists what it can do in one line, and offers taps that match the top reasons people message your page. One tap is always a way to reach a person.

Where do the taps come from? Your own inbox. Read the last 50 to 100 first messages on your page and group them. For most shops it is price, stock and delivery. For clinics it is booking and prices. For service businesses it is “how much” and “can you do this for me”. Those groups become your taps.

Here is an opener you can adapt. Every line is a template, not a real conversation:

Hi {first_name}, I'm the {business} assistant.
I can check prices and stock, book a visit, or connect you with our team.
What would you like to do?

[Prices and stock]  [Book a visit]  [Talk to a person]

How many quick replies should you offer?

Short answer: three to five, with short labels, even though Messenger allows more.

Messenger allows up to 13 quick replies on one message, and a text quick reply title can be up to 20 characters. Quick replies are also temporary: they disappear when the customer taps one and after the next message, so they suit one-time choices, not menus people return to. For reference, check the documentation here.

The limit is not the target. On a phone, five chips already wrap into rows, and every extra option is one more thing to read before the customer can act. Keep labels to two or three words, start with a verb or a noun the customer would use (“Prices”, “Book a visit”), and keep “Talk to a person” in the set.

Myth

More options make the bot more helpful.

Reality

More options make the first step harder. Cover the top reasons people message you, and let free text and a person handle the rest.

Quick reply, button or free text: which one when?

Short answer: quick replies for one-time choices, buttons for actions people may come back to, free text when the answer cannot fit in a tap.

UseWhenExample
Quick repliesA single choice that moves the conversation forward“Which branch is closest to you?”
ButtonsAn action that should stay visible on the message (a link, a call, a booking page)“See the price list”, “Call us”
Free textDetails that vary for every customer, or anything an AI agent should read in the customer's own words“Describe the problem with your unit”
Input fieldsA value you want to save to the contact recordPhone number, email, preferred date

If an AI agent handles the conversation, free text stops being a dead end, because the agent can read the customer's own words. Taps still matter: they are faster for the customer and they give the agent a clean signal of intent. The voice of those replies is its own topic, covered in how to make your chatbot sound human.

How many questions should a bot ask before the offer?

Short answer: only the questions your team needs to act, usually two or three, asked one at a time.

Every question should earn its place by changing what happens next: which product, which branch, how soon. If the answer would not change the reply or the routing, cut the question. Ask one thing per message, explain why when it is not obvious (“so I can check stock at the nearest branch”), and give the customer something useful right after they answer. The full method for scoring and routing those answers is in how to qualify leads in Messenger automatically.

How do you follow up without annoying people?

Short answer: one or two useful nudges inside the 24-hour window, each with a new reason to reply, and stop the moment the customer answers.

On Messenger, a business can reply freely for 24 hours after the customer's last message. Outside that window the options are narrow, and they narrowed further in 2026: Meta retired the POST_PURCHASE_UPDATE, CONFIRMED_EVENT_UPDATE and ACCOUNT_UPDATE message tags. The HUMAN_AGENT tag still lets a real person reply to a customer within 7 days, for genuine support cases, not mass messages. For order and shipping updates Meta points to utility message templates, and promotional messages go through opt-in marketing messages. For reference, check the summary of the change here.

So design your follow-ups to work inside the window. In Botcake AI, the agent's AI response behavior tab has an Auto follow-up switch that sends follow-ups when the customer has not replied, with a delay (“Send follow-up messages automatically after”) and a cap (“Number of messages”). You can let the AI write them or write them yourself. For flow-based bots, tag-based sequences do the same job: attach a sequence when a tag is added and remove it when the customer replies. For reference, check the documentation here.

What makes a follow-up welcome is the reason it gives. “Just checking in” adds nothing. An illustrative nudge like “The size you asked about is back in stock” or “Here is the price list you asked for” gives the customer a reason to answer. Each nudge should carry one new, useful thing and one easy tap. The longer-term follow-up system, including what happens after the window closes, is in how to automate lead follow-up with AI.

Myth

If the customer stops replying, the bot should keep messaging until they answer.

Reality

Messenger's rules limit what you can send after 24 hours, and silence is often an answer. Two useful nudges, then let a person decide.

Dijae's rule

Every bot message should end with one easy next step. If the customer has to work out what to type, the conversation is already leaking.

What can you set up today?

  1. Read your last 50 to 100 first messages and group them into the top three reasons people write.
  2. Rewrite your opener: who is talking, what it can do, three taps that match those reasons, plus “Talk to a person”.
  3. Trim every quick reply set to five options or fewer, with labels well under 20 characters.
  4. Cut every qualifying question that would not change the reply or the routing.
  5. Set follow-ups inside the 24-hour window, each with a new reason to reply, and stop on any reply.

Before you switch the new flow on, run it through the test cases in how to test an AI chatbot: every tap, every dead end, an upset customer and a customer who only types.

An honest limitation: a better script cannot fix a weak offer, wrong prices in the bot's knowledge or a team that takes hours to answer a handoff. Conversation design makes the path clear; the offer and the people behind it still decide the sale.

Frequently asked questions

What is chatbot conversation design?

It is planning what a chatbot says, which options it offers and when it follows up, so each message helps the customer take the next step. It covers the opener, quick replies and buttons, qualifying questions, follow-ups and the handoff to a person.

How many quick replies can a Messenger message have?

Messenger allows up to 13 quick replies per message, with titles of up to 20 characters. For most businesses, three to five short options convert better than a long list.

Can a chatbot message customers after 24 hours?

Only in limited cases. In 2026 Meta retired several Messenger message tags; a real person can still reply within 7 days using the HUMAN_AGENT tag, order updates move to utility templates and promotions need opt-in marketing messages. Plan routine follow-ups inside the 24-hour window.

What is a good chatbot opener example?

“Hi, I'm the shop's assistant. I can check prices and stock, book a visit, or connect you with our team. What would you like to do?” followed by three taps that match those options.
