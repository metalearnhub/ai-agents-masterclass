Most AI chatbots that disappoint were not built badly. They were launched untested, connected to nothing useful, or set up on a platform that did not fit how customers actually reach the business.

Imagine a clinic that turns on a chatbot on Friday. By Monday it has quoted an old price, booked a slot the doctor is not working, and kept an upset patient talking to a bot for ten minutes. None of that is an AI problem. It is a foundations problem.

The thread running through last Friday's guides: get the foundations right before you scale AI.

First, test it like a customer before a customer does. Real past questions, tricky edge cases and an upset caller, run before launch, catch most of the failures that cost trust. (How to test an AI chatbot)

Second, an assistant is only as useful as what it can reach. MCP is the open standard that lets AI assistants connect to your CRM, calendar and business tools, with permissions you control. (What is MCP, in plain English)

Third, the platform should match the channel. For Philippine and Southeast Asian businesses, the right choice depends on where customers already message you, your team and your budget. (The 2026 platform comparison)

One step for this week: pull 20 real questions from last month's messages and ask them to your chatbot, or to the one you plan to buy. Mark each answer right, wrong or "should have handed off." That list is your launch test and your shopping checklist in one.
